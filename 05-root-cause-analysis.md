05 — Root-Cause Analysis

1. Purpose

The purpose of this analysis is to investigate the potential underlying causes behind the process challenges identified in the current-state analysis.

The objective is to distinguish between visible symptoms and the underlying process, information, and decision-making issues that may contribute to them.

Note: Because this portfolio case study is not based on an internal audit or actual client interviews, the causes identified below are treated as hypotheses to be validated rather than confirmed facts about GlobalFawusi.

2. Core Problem

The central problem being investigated is:

GlobalFawusi needs a more structured and repeatable approach to assessing business needs and translating those assessments into prioritized, measurable recommendations.

3. 5 Whys Analysis

Problem: Business assessments may be inconsistent across engagements

Why 1 — Why might assessments be inconsistent?

Because different businesses may provide different information and consultants may approach assessments differently.

Why 2 — Why might consultants approach assessments differently?

Because there may not be one standardized assessment methodology covering every major business area.

Why 3 — Why might there not be a standardized methodology?

Because consulting engagements often require flexibility and may evolve according to the specific needs of each business.

Why 4 — Why does this create a challenge?

Because flexibility without a common analytical structure can make it difficult to compare assessments, identify recurring patterns, or consistently prioritize problems.

Why 5 — Why is this important as GlobalFawusi grows?

Because a scalable consulting platform needs enough standardization to maintain consistency while still allowing recommendations to be customized for individual businesses.

Root Cause Hypothesis

The underlying issue may be the absence of a sufficiently standardized assessment and analysis framework that balances consistency with the flexibility required for different businesses.

4. Cause-and-Effect Analysis

The potential causes can be grouped into several categories.

People

Potential contributing factors:

* Different levels of consulting experience
* Different approaches to diagnosing business problems
* Different interpretations of client needs
* Limited standardized guidance for assessments

Process

Potential contributing factors:

* No clearly defined assessment sequence
* Inconsistent information collection
* Limited formal problem-prioritization process
* Recommendations may not always follow a standardized methodology
* Follow-up measurement may vary between engagements

Data

Potential contributing factors:

* Incomplete business information
* Different businesses track different metrics
* Limited historical performance data
* Data may be stored in different formats
* Lack of standardized business-health indicators

Technology

Potential contributing factors:

* Limited centralization of assessment information
* Manual handling of some business information
* Limited analytical dashboards or reporting tools
* Limited automation of recurring analysis

Business Environment

Potential contributing factors:

* MSMEs operate across different industries
* Businesses have different levels of maturity
* Available resources vary significantly
* Business priorities can change quickly
* Some entrepreneurs may have limited formal business records

5. Symptoms vs. Root Causes

Observed / Potential Symptom	Possible Underlying Cause
Different information collected from businesses	Lack of standardized assessment structure
Difficulty comparing businesses	Inconsistent data collection and metrics
Too many problems identified at once	Lack of formal prioritization framework
Recommendations vary between engagements	Lack of standardized analytical methodology
Difficulty measuring outcomes	Recommendations not consistently linked to KPIs
Difficulty scaling consulting	Heavy dependence on manual or individual approaches

6. Root-Cause Summary

The analysis suggests that the central opportunity is not simply to collect more information.

Instead, GlobalFawusi may benefit from creating a structured system that connects:

Business Information → Problem Identification → Root-Cause Analysis → Prioritization → Recommendation → KPI → Follow-Up

This would allow the consulting process to remain customized while introducing a consistent analytical foundation.

7. Key Insight

The root problem appears to be a process and information-structure challenge rather than a single business-function problem.

Therefore, the proposed solution should not focus only on one area such as marketing, finance, or customer acquisition.

Instead, it should provide a broader framework for assessing businesses and identifying which specific areas require intervention.

8. Validation Required

Before implementing the proposed framework in a real consulting environment, the following assumptions should be validated through:

* Interviews with GlobalFawusi consultants
* Review of previous consulting engagements
* Review of existing assessment methods
* Interviews or surveys with entrepreneurs and MSMEs
* Analysis of historical business-performance information
* Review of how recommendations and outcomes are currently documented

The validation stage is important because the root causes identified in this portfolio project are analytical hypotheses rather than confirmed internal findings.
